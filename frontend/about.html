Project Overview

The Science Behind FIRE-X

Why Microgravity & Partial Gravity?

In microgravity (~0g), buoyancy-driven convection is absent: flames become spherical, low-temperature cool flames (600–800 K) persist after visible quenching, and low ventilation flows (5–15 cm/s) make thin materials MORE flammable than on Earth. Because there is currently no raw partial-gravity flight data in NASA PSI (pending future LUCI and FM2 lunar investigations), FIRE-X bounds Lunar (0.16g) and Martian (0.38g) hazards between 0g ISS forced-flow data and 1.0g NASA-STD-6001 Test 1 upward screening, supplemented by ACME E-FIELD electric-body-force partial-gravity mimicry.

The 892-Test Canonical Dataset

892 canonical experiments across 29 NASA PSI & WSTF investigations: FLEX-1/2 (spherical droplets + CO₂ up to 70% & He up to 45% diluent suppression), BASS-I/II, SPICE, and SLICE (in ISS MSG flow duct), SAFFIRE I–VI (large-scale Cygnus spacecraft burns), ACME Suite (BRE, CFI, CFI-G up to 85% O₂ inside-out flames, CLD, E-FIELD in ISS CIR), SOFIE, SAME / SAME-R, DAFT / DAFT-2 (lunar/Martian dust vs smoke discrimination), and NASA-STD-6001 (1.0g WSTF upper bound).

Live SQLite SQL-RAG + OpenAI GPT-4o-mini

The platform pairs OpenAI GPT-4o-mini with a live SQLite SQL-RAG Retrieval Engine querying fire_safety.db on every prompt. All AI calls are server-side only — the API key is never exposed to the browser, and every answer cites live SQL telemetry rows and investigation rankings.

Mission Simulator & Scope Exclusion

The Fire Hazard Index formula FHI = min(10, 5.1 × (X_O2/0.21)^1.5 × (P/101.3)^0.4 × M_f) models Spacecraft, Space Station (ISS), Lunar Base (0.16g), Martian Base (0.38g), Pressurized Rover (≥30% O₂, ≤10.2 psi), and EVA Spacesuit (85–100% O₂, 4.3 psi). Scope Note: Rocket engine propellants (LOX/LH₂, hypergolics) are intentionally excluded because high-pressure turbulent atomization (>50 bar, Fr ≫ 1000) is buoyancy-independent, unlike low-velocity spacecraft cabin fires.

Spaceflight & Terrestrial Impact

3-Tier Fire Safety Equipment Impact & Earth Spin-Offs

TIER 1 // FIRE PREVENTION

Material Screening & Airflow Control

In Space: BASS-II, SOFIE, and SAFFIRE revealed that thin materials self-extinguish in 0g stagnant air when ventilation fans are shut off, yet burn at lower O₂ limits under 5–15 cm/s forced flow (and 0.16g Lunar buoyancy) than at 1.0g. This reshaped material certification for Artemis Lunar Bases, Pressurized Rovers, and EVA Spacesuits.

On Earth: Drives ultra-high-LOI PBI/Nomex/Kevlar firefighter turnout gear, aircraft cabin fire barriers, and lightweight composite SCBA breathing cylinders.

TIER 2 // FIRE DETECTION

Smoke Aging & Lunar Dust Immunity

In Space: SAME and SAME-R proved microgravity smoke particles grow into larger chain-like aggregates due to longer flame residence time. DAFT and DAFT-2 used SAME hardware in ISS MSG to calibrate multi-moment optical scattering ratios so Lunar/Martian habitat and Rover smoke detectors distinguish coarse regolith dust (>1 µm) from sub-micron pyrolysis smoke (<0.5 µm).

On Earth: Powers false-alarm-immune dual-wavelength residential, hospital, and industrial mine smoke detectors.

TIER 3 // FIRE SUPPRESSION

He / CO₂ Diluents & Fine Water Mist

In Space: FLEX (PSI-69) quantified droplet quenching across 123 CO₂ (up to 70%) and 50 Helium (He, up to 45%) tests — proving Helium's 6× higher thermal diffusivity rapidly quenches both hot and 600–800 K cool flames while NASA's Portable Fine Water Mist (PFWM) extinguishers avoid cabin CO₂ toxicity.

On Earth: Clean fine-water-mist and inert-gas suppression systems protecting server farms, museums, telecommunications hubs, and submarines.
